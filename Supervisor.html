Weather Conditions & Force Majeure

Authority and conduct — Section 3 of the Handbook.

Authority

The CEV Supervisor has the authority to suspend the competition if weather or other Force Majeure has a direct impact on the tournament, until normal conditions are re-established. Before stopping a match/event for safety reasons, the Supervisor must consult the LOC and inform the CEV Head Office.

Light / moderate rain

Tournaments will not be stopped or cancelled. Play should only be suspended in case of thunder and/or lightning in the area where the event is held. Postponement, rescheduling, short delays or cancellation may apply in case of thunderstorms, hurricanes, flooding and other dangerous conditions.

Lightning & inclement weather protocol

  • All participants, team staff, spectators, staff and volunteers must immediately evacuate the beach and go to cars or indoor facilities — do not seek shelter under trees.
  • To resume play, the area must be free of lightning for at least 30 minutes.
  • Count the seconds between lightning and thunder. More than 30 seconds = safe to play. Less than 30 seconds = seek shelter.

Case book — "Both captains request the 1st referee to stop play during heavy rain"

The decision to stop playing is made by the Beach Volleyball Organising Committee (Supervisor/TD, National Federation Delegate and Tournament Director). Only if they are unavailable and the referee foresees significant danger to players from lightning or sudden wind gusts, can the referee stop play and send players to their time-out areas.

Webinar reminders

  • Anticipate — monitor forecasts ahead of time.
  • Stay in close collaboration with the LOC and Volleyball World — safety first.
  • Keep a constant flow of information to CEV, referees, teams and spectators (Slack / Live Chat / announcer).

Data Collection & Match Technology

Section 8 of the Handbook — VIS, CEV WCM, Match State Platform, E-scoresheet, Click&Scout Media and Live Chat.

The chain

VIS → CEV WCM → Match State Platform (Genius Sports) → E-scoresheet + Click&Scout Media. All Beach competitions are primarily managed in and through the VIS. All matches should be scouted with both data-collection tools simultaneously.

VIS

  • Update match info (time, teams, referees, etc.) as soon as possible — sync to MSP can take up to 30 minutes.
  • Avoid changing the tournament match template — it requires manual rework. If unavoidable, report via Live Chat immediately, mentioning the competition gender.
  • After Preliminary Inquiry matches are added, inform CEV and Genius Sports via Live Chat.
  • Enter a match result manually only if E-scoresheet did not finalise or sync — report on Live Chat with the detailed match score.
  • For a no-start / injury forfeit: enter the score at the moment of the injury (for correct rally-point ratio); for a genuine no-start use 0–0, 0–0 and override the automatic 21–0, 21–0.
  • Matches must be closed in VIS within 30 seconds of the end of the match.

E-scoresheet

  • Windows 10/11 laptops only (Android/MacBook not supported).
  • Wired LAN connection from start until the match is finalised (Wi-Fi only for underage completions).
  • Minimum internet speed 10 Mb/s upload & download.
  • Update to the latest version a day before the tournament starts.
  • Open every match with its unique match key; open and finalise on-site at the court, within 5 minutes of the match ending.
  • Devices need sufficient power (charger or a fully charged swap). The LED box cannot be connected to the scoring device.
  • Results and the scoresheet return to VIS within up to 15 minutes after the match is finalised.

Click & Scout Media

  • Windows 8.1+ (or MacBook) — meet the minimum requirements.
  • Minimum 10 Mb/s connection (LAN or Wi-Fi).
  • Update to the latest version a day before the tournament and check "New Trial Match" opens without issue.

Live Chat (Genius Sports Helpdesk)

  • Create an account and inform data@cev.eu before the competition.
  • Available from 2 days before to 1 day after the competition dates.
  • Be precise: state the application name, match/teams and time, and attach screenshots for every issue.

Sportradar scouts (new for 2026)

Where CEV confirms Sportradar scouts will attend, the LOC must provide: free access to the venue, one seat per match court with good visibility and power supply, and Wi-Fi with a minimum 20 Mb/s upload/download at the data-collection position on every court. Scout details are shared with the organiser roughly one week before the tournament, with the Supervisor in copy.

Fines, Sanctions & Protests

Section 9 of the Handbook — Competition Operations reference tables.

Free zone

Per FIVB Rules of the Game. In Futures and Age Group tournaments, side-court size can be reduced due to natural/physical obstructions — a minimum of 3 m free zone (instead of 5 m) is acceptable for side courts.

For Volleyball World Futures in Europe: after 16:00 CET the Friday before tournament week, teams may submit BVB/03 (withdrawal) or BVB/04 (athlete change) forms to withdraw/change composition — a monetary fine still applies: USD 200/athlete (Main Draw) or USD 100/athlete (Qualification).

Protest fee

A deposit of €300 is required for any protest raised in a CEV competition. If the protest is rejected (on the merits or on form), the fee below applies. On a side court, the amounts are halved; for repeat protests by the same team, each subsequent fee doubles.

CEV CompetitionProtest fee
CEV EuroBeachVolley / European Championship€500
CEV Beach Volleyball Nations Cup Final€500
Other CEV competitions€250

For Futures: progressive fee — USD 500 first time at a tournament, minimum USD 1,000 second time (doubling after), or USD 1,000 / min. USD 2,500 if on Centre Court, when the Protest Protocol is judged "Rejected" by the FIVB Referee Coach/Technical Delegate.

Action: always tell the player a protest costs money, show the amount, then ask if they still want to proceed.
